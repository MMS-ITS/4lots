# Audit — "Bar X Ranch · 4-Lot Feasibility Portfolio"

**Subject:** the 9-page generated artifact titled *Bar X Ranch – 4-Lot Feasibility Portfolio · 5-Bedroom Build + Fishing Hobby + Flood Risk Analysis*
**Audit date:** 10 September 2026
**Method:** the artifact's own sections were compared against each other, then every material technical and financial claim was tested against primary sources (TCEQ regulation text, Brazoria County's published fee schedules and permit forms, FEMA, TWIA, TPWD, Brazoria County Truth-in-Taxation filings). Sources are listed in [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md).

> **A note on "compare the documents."** Only one document was supplied. What follows therefore compares the artifact's **internal sections** against one another — hero metrics vs. lot cards vs. flood summary vs. buildability matrix vs. cost schedule vs. e-mail drafts vs. the two elevation diagrams vs. the sources block — and then against external primary sources. Several of the most serious contradictions are *between sections of the same document*. If you meant a second document (an appraisal, a survey, a seller disclosure, the POA restrictions), send it and this audit will be extended.

## 1. Verdict: what the document concludes, and why that conclusion does not hold

The artifact's operative verdict is carried by four green **"Buildable"** badges — one per lot — plus the closing line *"all meet 5BR footprint"* and a soft-cost total of *"~$11k–$17k"* with fill described as *"(minimal)."* Read together, the document says: **all four lots work, the flood zone is a paperwork problem rather than a cost problem, and the money required before you build is about the price of a used car.**

That verdict fails on five independent grounds.

### 1.1 The document argues against itself on risk

The artifact opens with a section headed *"Why 'No Flood Risk' Is Impossible"* — a correct and useful rebuttal of a listing claim. It then spends the remaining eight pages neutralising its own warning: *"+4 ft free freeboard," "positive natural freeboard," "minimal"* fill, uniform "Buildable" badges, and a finished-floor requirement it presents as easily satisfied. The document establishes that the risk is real and then prices it at nearly zero. Both halves cannot be right.

### 1.2 The entire flood conclusion rests on the most optimistic reading of a 2.7-foot range

The artifact states the governing hydrology as *"AD 23.7 ft → AE 26.4 ft"* and then interpolates **"site BFE ~24 ft."** Nothing in the document justifies landing at 24 — the bottom of the range. The midpoint would be ~25.05 ft; the upstream value is 26.4 ft.

This single unjustified choice is load-bearing for everything else:

| If site BFE is… | Required finished floor (BFE + 2 ft) | Versus natural ground 28–30 ft | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24.0 ft *(document's figure)* | 26.0 ft | 2–4 ft **below** grade | Requirement met by building at grade. No fill. LOMA plausible. |
| 25.0 ft (midpoint) | 27.0 ft | 1–3 ft below grade | Still met at grade. |
| **26.4 ft (upstream section)** | **28.4 ft** | **at or above grade on a 28 ft lot** | **Fill or stem wall required; LOMA materially weaker; the "minimal" cost premise collapses.** |

The document never runs this sensitivity. A feasibility study whose favourable conclusion depends on picking the low end of its own stated range, without stating why, has not established feasibility — it has expressed a preference.

### 1.3 "AD" and "AE" are being used to mean two different things

The document uses **AE** as a flood *zone* throughout (hero card, every lot card, every badge) while simultaneously using **AD** and **AE** as *lettered cross-sections* on the FIS flood profile (*"AD 23.7 ft – AE 26.4 ft"*). These are unrelated concepts, and **there is no Zone AD in the NFIP.** As written, a reader — including the floodplain administrator who receives the draft e-mails — cannot tell whether the document is quoting zones or cross-sections. This terminology collision is repeated in the hero card, the flood summary, the BFE diagram caption, and the sources block.

### 1.4 One generic flood analysis is presented as four lot-specific analyses

All four lot cards carry **identical** flood data: `AE • BFE ~24 ft NAVD88 • Ground 28-30 ft • +4 ft nat. freeboard • Panel 0605K`. Four parcels, in at least two different sections of a large subdivision, cannot all share an identical 2-foot ground-elevation band and an identical interpolated BFE. This is one analysis copy-pasted four times.

Corroborating evidence that the lots do **not** share one hydrology: a neighbouring listing on the same street as Lot 1 describes *views of Mill Bayou* — a different water body from the *Bastrop Bayou West Tributary* the document assigns to all four lots. Different contributing stream, different flood profile, different BFE.

### 1.5 The waterfront lots — the document's own "best picks" — are the weakest lots for this build, not the strongest

The artifact recommends *"1127 Saddle Horn (1.95 AC waterfront) + 336 Wagon Wheel W (cleared waterfront, 1 AC)"* as the best picks, and gives them the same "Buildable" badge as everything else. Two facts the document never surfaces run directly against that:

1. **Waterfront lots slope down to the water.** The lake-side portion of a waterfront lot is necessarily the low point and is the most likely part of any of these parcels to sit *below* BFE. Yet the document assigns waterfront lots the same flat "+4 ft" cushion as the interior lot. A LOMA turns on the **lowest adjacent grade** at the structure, not on the lot's high point — so the waterfront lots are the ones where the "+4 ft" claim is least likely to survive a survey.
2. **TCEQ separation distances bite hardest on a 1-acre waterfront lot.** A private well must sit **100 ft** from the soil-absorption area or spray field, and the absorption area must sit **75 ft** back from the normal pool elevation of a lake (30 TAC §285.91, Table X). Stack those against a house, a garage, a driveway, a 2,250–4,500 sq ft drainfield and its reserve, and the geometry on a one-acre waterfront lot is genuinely tight.

The document asserts *"1.95 AC lot easiest for setback + reserve"* and *"all meet 5BR footprint"* — while citing **not one setback dimension anywhere in nine pages.** The conclusion is stated; the analysis behind it is absent.

### 1.6 The document never answers the question it exists to answer

The hero metric is **"TOTAL PORTFOLIO $234,500 · 4 lots."** Every other section is a comparison written to help choose *one* lot. These are different documents:

- If the intent is **buy one of four**, the deliverable needs a ranked recommendation and a decision matrix. It has neither — instead, three sections nominate three *different* winners (fishing → Saddle Horn + 336; buildability → 1.95 AC lot; privacy → Lot 29), and no section reconciles them.
- If the intent is **buy all four for $234,500**, the deliverable needs an investment thesis, a carrying-cost model, and a resale view — and the 5-bedroom buildability work is largely moot, since you would build on one lot. It has none of these, and **no carrying costs at all** (see §4.1).

**This unresolved premise is the single largest defect in the document.** Everything downstream inherits it.

Lot 3 is badged **"CHEAPEST WATERFRONT"** at $45,000. Lot 1 is also waterfront and is cheaper *per acre* ($42,308). The badge is true on absolute price and false on unit price, and the document does not say which it means — while elsewhere leaning on $/acre as its comparison metric.

### 2.5 The fill line item contradicts the document's own elevation finding

The cost schedule reads: *"Add fill if needed to hit 26 ft FF: ~$15–$25/cy, est 80–150 cy = $1,200–$3,750."*

The document states natural ground is **28–30 ft** and required finished floor is **26 ft**. **You cannot need fill to reach an elevation 2–4 ft below where the ground already is.** As written, the line item is incoherent on the document's own numbers. (Fill may well be needed — for a crowned pad, drainage, and driveway — but that is a different justification, and 80–150 cy is roughly 1–1.8 ft over the building footprint *only*, with no allowance for side slopes or over-build.)

### 2.6 Both elevation diagrams draw the finished floor below the ground

The page-5 diagram places **"FINISHED FLOOR 26 ft"** below **"Natural Ground 28-30 ft."** The page-6 "FEMA REFERENCE • OFFICIAL DIAGRAM" does the same, and states it in words in its key: *"Ground elevation = 28 ft NAVD88 … Finished floor at 26 ft meets requirement."* Both diagrams depict a house whose floor is two feet underground. The diagrams are not merely unclear — they are physically impossible, and they are the document's primary visual explanation of its central technical finding.

The correct framing, which the document never states: **at 28–30 ft natural grade, the county's 26 ft minimum is not the binding constraint.** The finished floor will land at roughly 28.5–31 ft simply by building at grade. That is *good news the document fails to claim*, and it is the actual answer to "how high must I build."

### 2.7 Fill and a LOMA are mutually exclusive — the document proposes both

The artifact recommends pursuing a **LOMA** (*"Natural 28-30 ft may qualify for Letter of Map Amendment"*) and, three lines earlier in the cost schedule, recommends **placing fill**. FEMA's definition of a LOMA is a determination for land *"on naturally high ground and has not been elevated by fill."* Place fill under the structure and the instrument becomes a **LOMR-F**, which is a different application with a different evidentiary burden and — unlike a LOMA — a FEMA review fee. The document's two recommendations cancel each other.

### 2.8 Section coverage does not match the subtitle

The subtitle claims *"Bar X Ranch Sec 2 / Sec 16."* Actual card contents: Lot 1 "Bar X Ranch Sec 2"; Lot 2 "Bar X Ranch" + "0140"; Lot 3 "Lot 167 Acres 1 • Bar X Ranch"; Lot 4 "Bar X Ranch Sec 16". **Two of four lots have no section identified**, yet the document's title asserts section coverage for all four — and then applies a single set of deed restrictions ("ACC Rev 2") across all of them. See §3.5.

### 2.9 HOA dues are stated for one lot and omitted for three

Lot 3 alone carries *"Annual $250" / "$250/yr HOA."* Lots 1, 2 and 4 show only *"HOA"* or *"HOA true"* with no figure. All four are in the same association; the dues should be stated for all four or for none. The figure itself is also wrong — see §3.6.

### 2.10 The four "tailored templates" are structurally inconsistent and truncated

The e-mail section promises *"4 tailored templates."* What is delivered:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 3. Technical claims tested against primary sources

### 3.1 Septic: right answer, wrong derivation, and the working notes were left in

The document prints this, verbatim, in the finished artifact:

> *"Septic 5BR sizing: Q = 360 gpd (5BR × 90?) per TCEQ – actually 240 gpd base + 120 per extra BR → 600 gpd? Local requires 1,250 gal tank (1,000 + 250). Drainfield ~0.6 gpd/sf."*

Two candidate flow rates, both with question marks, and a rule that is not Texas's. Against **30 TAC §285.91**:

| Claim | Status | Correct position |
|---|---|---|
| Q = 360 gpd | **Partly right** | Table III: 5 BR under 4,500 sf = **450 gpd** without water-saving devices, **360 gpd** with them. 360 is the *concessional* figure, not the default. |
| "240 gpd base + 120 per extra BR → 600 gpd?" | **Wrong** | Not a TCEQ rule. Discard. |
| Tank = 1,250 gal | **Correct** | But *not* via "1,000 + 250 for the 5th BR" (an IPC/UPC-style rule). §285.91(2): Q of 351–500 gpd → **V = 1,250 gal**. Both 450 and 360 gpd land in that band, so 1,250 gal is robust — the document got there by luck. |
| "1,000 + 250" derivation | **Wrong** | Cite the flow band, not a per-bedroom increment. |

### 3.2 Septic: the drainfield is undersized by 3–6× and the system type is probably wrong

This is the most consequential technical error in the document.

**"Drainfield ~0.6 gpd/sf"** — TCEQ Table I caps long-term application rate at **>0.50 gal/sf/day for Class Ia soil**, the most permeable sand in the table. **0.6 is off the top of the table entirely.** It is not a conservative figure or an aggressive figure; it is not a figure the regulation contains.

Absorptive area = Q ÷ Ra. At Q = 450 gpd:

| Soil class | Ra (gal/sf/day) | Required absorptive area |
|---|---|---|
| Ia | >0.50 | ~900 sq ft |
| Ib | 0.38 | ~1,184 sq ft |
| II | 0.25 | ~1,800 sq ft |
| III | 0.20 | **~2,250 sq ft** |
| IV | 0.10 | **~4,500 sq ft** |

The document says **"~600-800 sq ft."** Brazoria County's coastal-prairie soils are heavy clay — Class III–IV territory. On the realistic classes the requirement is **2,250–4,500 sq ft: three to six times what the document budgeted**, before the reserve area it also claims.

Worse, **Class IV clay generally cannot use a conventional absorptive drainfield at all.** The likely permitted system is an **aerobic treatment unit with spray or drip irrigation** — a different system, with a different tank (Table: 5 BR under 4,501 sf → **600-gallon minimum aerobic capacity**, not 1,250 gal), a much higher installed cost, and a **TCEQ-mandated ongoing maintenance contract** the document never mentions. The document's stray *"600 gpd?"* appears to be this 600-gallon aerobic figure, misread as a flow rate.

**And the installed cost of the septic system is missing from the cost schedule entirely.** The schedule budgets the *permit* ($600–$900) and the *soil test* ($350), then stops. The system itself — $8,000–$15,000 conventional, **$15,000–$25,000 aerobic with spray field** — does not appear anywhere in nine pages.

### 3.3 Setbacks: cited nowhere, and they govern the very claim the document makes

Zero separation distances appear in the document, which nonetheless concludes *"all meet 5BR footprint."* The binding numbers, 30 TAC §285.91 Table X:

| From | To | Minimum |
|---|---|---|
| Private well / cistern | Soil absorption system | **100 ft** |
| Private well / cistern | Surface (spray) application area | **100 ft** |
| Private well / cistern | Tank | 50 ft |
| Lake / pond *(from normal pool elevation)* | Soil absorption system, unlined ET bed | **75 ft** |
| Lake / pond | Tank | 50 ft |
| Lake / pond | Surface application area | 50 ft |
| Lake / pond | Drip irrigation | 25 ft (Ra < 0.1) / 75 ft (Ra > 0.1) |

On a one-acre waterfront lot these constraints, plus a 2,250–4,500 sq ft field and its reserve, plus house, garage and driveway, are the real feasibility question for this programme. The document does not pose it.

### 3.4 County permit fees are overstated by roughly 7–11×, and double-counted

Brazoria County's published schedule (*Building Permit Fees*, approved 9 September 2015) sets **residential, in a flood zone**, at **$75 permit fee + $0.04 per sq ft of enclosed area (home and garage only)**.

For 1,800 sf living + 440 sf garage = 2,240 sf → **$75 + $89.60 = $164.60.**

| Document | Actual | Delta |
|---|---|---|
| "Floodplain Development Permit $150–$300" + "Building Permit (1,800 sf + garage) $1,200–$1,800" | One combined development/building permit: **~$165** | **Overstated ~$1,185–$1,935** |

Two further errors compound it:

- **Double-counting.** Brazoria County issues a single combined *Development/Building Permit* — that is the literal title of the county's application form. The document invoices it twice under two names.
- **The $1,200–$1,800 figure appears to be borrowed from the commercial tier** ($275 permit + $100 inspection + $0.04/sf), or from a municipal valuation-based fee model. It does not correspond to any residential line on the county schedule.
- **Omitted:** the separate **Fill & Grading permit**, $50 + $30 inspection = **$80** in a flood zone — which the document needs, because it proposes fill.

### 3.5 "Zoning 0140" is not zoning

The document prints **"SUBDIVISION / ZONING"** headers and populates them with `0140`, `Single-Family • HOA`, `Cul-de-sac • HOA true`. Then it repeats `Zoning 0140` twice in a draft e-mail **to the county**.

**Texas counties have no general zoning authority outside municipal limits.** There is no county zoning on these lots. `0140` is a **Brazoria County Appraisal District state land-use code**, not a zoning designation. What actually controls use here is the recorded **deed restrictions** and the POA's architectural control committee — a different instrument with a different enforcement mechanism. Sending "Zoning 0140" to the floodplain administrator signals that the sender does not know which regime applies.

Relatedly, the document applies **one** restrictive covenant set — *"1,800 sq ft min + 22×20 garage, ACC Rev 2"* — uniformly across lots in **Section 2 and Section 16**. Bar X Ranch sections have **separately recorded** restrictions, and minimum square footage commonly differs between them. The document presents a cross-section generalisation as verified fact for all four lots, citing a single document it never identifies by recording reference.

### 3.6 POA dues are understated by roughly half, and the vacant-lot fee is missing

The document: **"$250 annual"** on one lot, silence on three.

Marketing material for Bar X Ranch states the POA fee is **$400 per lot per year, plus an additional $125 vacant-lot mowing fee** — i.e. **~$525 per year per vacant lot.** All four subject lots are vacant.

| | Document implies | Likely actual |
|---|---:|---:|
| Per vacant lot / yr | $250 (one lot only) | **~$525** |
| Four vacant lots / yr | not stated | **~$2,100** |

*(Third-party listing data — must be confirmed against the POA resale certificate. But the document's single $250 figure is not defensible as printed.)*

The document also under-describes what the dues buy: it lists only *"2 lakes • pier • ramp."* Bar X Ranch is marketed with **two swimming pools, two clubhouses, tennis and basketball courts, two playgrounds, pavilions, boat ramp and fishing pier.** For a document whose stated purpose includes a recreational hobby, omitting most of the amenity base is a material gap — and it is what explains the $400 dues the document didn't find.

### 3.7 The Harvey causation claim is false

The document asserts twice: *"Harvey (2017) showed levee overtopping risk; county enforces freeboard regardless"* and *"Harvey 2017: Levee overtopping + sheet flow documented → county raised freeboard to 2 ft."*

**Brazoria County Commissioners Court set the two-foot standard in May 2005** — twelve years before Harvey. Harvey did not produce it. The freeboard requirement is real and correctly stated as 24 inches; the causal story attached to it is invented. That matters because the document uses the Harvey narrative to justify its risk framing, and a floodplain administrator will recognise the error immediately.

### 3.8 LOMA cost and effect are both misstated

| Document | Correct position |
|---|---|
| "LOMA … ~$500-$900 + survey" | **FEMA charges no review fee for a LOMA.** The cost is the surveyor's/engineer's preparation of the elevation data and MT-EZ/MT-1 package — roughly $500–$1,200. A **LOMR-F** (required if fill is placed) *does* carry a FEMA fee. |
| "flood insurance $600-$1,800/yr est. before LOMA" | A LOMA lifts the **federal mandatory-purchase requirement**; it does not end the exposure, and a lender may still require coverage. Framing insurance as a pre-LOMA cost only implies it disappears after. It shouldn't. |
| "Insurance + elevation cert required" | Mandatory purchase attaches only where there is a **federally backed mortgage**. A cash purchase carries no federal mandate — the risk is unchanged, the obligation is not. The elevation certificate is required by the **county** for its permit regardless. |

Also unmentioned: under **Risk Rating 2.0**, premiums are not driven primarily by zone and BFE but by replacement cost, first-floor height and **distance to water** — which makes the *waterfront* lots the more expensive ones to insure, cutting directly against the document's recommendation. And **NFIP building coverage caps at $250,000**, well short of the house this document contemplates; excess flood cover is required and unbudgeted.

### 3.9 Windstorm is absent, and it is probably the largest recurring cost in the project

Nine pages on coastal Brazoria County contain **no mention of windstorm insurance, WPI-8 certification, or TDI.**

- Brazoria County is a **first-tier TDI-designated catastrophe area.**
- **WPI-8 certification is required for insurability**, and for **new construction it must be engineer-certified** — a design and inspection obligation running alongside the build, not a formality.
- The **TWIA average residential premium is approximately $2,541/year** (as of 30 June 2026). Brazoria County alone carries ~58,000 TWIA policies against ~$25.7 bn of exposure.

The document's entire insurance discussion is a flood range of *"$600–$1,800/yr."* The omitted windstorm line is, on average, **larger than the flood figure the document does show.** Realistic all-in annual insurance once built — flood + windstorm + homeowners ex-wind ex-flood — is on the order of **$5,000–$9,500**, i.e. roughly **3–5× what the document implies.**

### 3.10 Fishing: the state-limit claim and the private-water claim contradict each other, and the flood finding undermines both

The hero card reads **"Bass / Catfish • 10/day • No license on private."**

TPWD's position: *"A fishing license is not required to fish on waters completely enclosed within private property"* — and, where a pond is self-contained, **no size or bag limits apply either.**

So the two halves of the hero card cannot both be regulatory: **if no licence is required because the water is private, then no state bag limit applies, and "10/day" can only be a POA house rule** — which the document neither says nor sources. Meanwhile the statewide limit for blue/channel catfish is generally **25/day combined** (flathead 5), so 10 is not the state number either. The card also implies 10/day applies to bass; the statewide bass limit is 5.

The sharper problem is that **the document's own flood analysis undercuts its fishing claim.** TPWD's private-water exemption is conditional on the water body being self-contained and *not subject to overflow from public water*. These lots are in the **Bastrop Bayou watershed, mapped Zone AE — a 1% annual-chance floodplain**, which is precisely a statement that public water inundates the area. Whether the Bar X lakes qualify as private water is therefore a genuine open question, and the document's own central finding is the reason to ask it.

Two further data problems in the same section: **black drum** is a saltwater/brackish species and is listed as an on-site species in a private freshwater lake — almost certainly a bad data pull from crowd-sourced app data; and **gar** is listed among *stocked* species, which is not a thing anyone does. *"Surfside Beach – 20 min drive"* is also optimistic; local material for this subdivision says **~30 minutes to the beach.**

### 3.11 750 Wagon Wheel Trail may be waterfront — the document says it is not

The document badges Lot 2 **"ROAD FRONTAGE"** and describes it as *"Interior lot with road frontage"*, withholding the "Waterfront" badge it grants two other lots.

The active listing for 750 Wagon Wheel Trl describes it as a **"Premium location waterfront property … spacious, cleared waterfront lot"** in Bar X Ranch, and refers to *"the lake house build of your dreams."*

If the listing is right, then:
- **Three of four lots are waterfront**, not two.
- **"CHEAPEST WATERFRONT"** on Lot 3 is still defensible on price, but the waterfront comparison set is wrong.
- The fishing recommendation — which names two "best picks" for lake access — **omits a third waterfront lot**.
- Lot 2's classification, its comparison group, and its valuation narrative all change.

This is a factual conflict between the document and the marketing of record, on a binary attribute, and it must be resolved by survey and plat rather than by listing copy.

### 3.12 The elevation source is asserted, not shown

*"Ground ~28-30 ft natural per county LIDAR"* appears once, in the sources block. No spot elevations, no lowest-adjacent-grade figure, no date or vintage of the LIDAR, and — as noted in §1.4 — one identical band applied to four parcels. Two further considerations are absent that specifically threaten a "+4 ft cushion" over a 30-year hold on the Texas Gulf Coast:

- **Subsidence.** Documented across the Houston–Brazoria coastal plain. Historic LIDAR can overstate *present* ground elevation, and the cushion erodes without anyone doing anything.
- **Relative sea-level rise**, among the highest rates in the United States at Freeport, with tidal influence up Bastrop Bayou.

A static "+4 ft" is not a durable finding on this coastline; it is a snapshot presented as a permanent margin.

### 3.13 Pluvial flooding is named once and then dropped

The document mentions *"sheet flow documented"* in a single Harvey bullet and never returns to it. On flat coastal-prairie clay, **rainfall-driven ponding and sheet flow are the dominant local flood mechanism**, and much of the Harvey damage in this area was pluvial rather than riverine. A riverine 1%-annual-chance BFE does not describe that risk at all. A lot can sit comfortably above BFE and still pond. The document's entire risk model is riverine, and it says so nowhere.

## 4. What is missing

### 4.1 Carrying cost — completely absent

No property tax appears anywhere in nine pages. ## 7. What to do next, in order

1. **Decide the premise.** One lot or four? Nothing else can be sized until this is answered. *(§1.6)*
2. **Get parcel identifiers** for all four lots from BCAD — account number, legal description, acreage of record, plat. Without these, every figure in the document is unauditable. *(§4.5)*
3. **Confirm jurisdiction** — unincorporated county vs Angleton city limits, per parcel. *(§4.5)*
4. **Request a written BFE determination per lot** from Brazoria County Floodplain & 911 Administration, 451 N Velasco Ste 210, Angleton TX 77515 · 979-864-1295 (also 281-756-1295). Ask which cross-section governs each lot and how it was interpolated — and ask specifically whether the governing stream is Bastrop Bayou West Tributary or Mill Bayou. *(§1.3, §1.4)*
5. **Resolve Lot 2's frontage and acreage** — waterfront or interior, 1.0 or 1.27 acres. Both are binary facts settled by the plat. *(§2.2, §3.11)*
6. **Order a soil/site evaluation on the shortlisted lot before anything else.** Soil class decides conventional vs aerobic septic, which is the single largest swing in the pre-construction budget and the largest constraint on lot layout. *(§3.2)*
7. **Order a boundary + topographic survey** with spot elevations and lowest adjacent grade, then test the septic/well/lake setback geometry against a real 5-bedroom footprint. *(§3.3)*
8. **Get three quotes each** for well, septic, and electric service extension. These three lines carry most of the uncertainty. *(§4.2)*
9. **Get a TWIA/windstorm quote and a Risk Rating 2.0 flood quote** on the actual proposed structure, and price excess flood above the $250k NFIP cap. *(§3.9, §3.8)*
10. **Pull the recorded restrictions for the specific section** of each shortlisted lot, plus a POA resale certificate showing dues, vacant-lot fees, transfer fees, and the actual lake rules. Stop relying on "ACC Rev 2" as a single document. *(§3.5, §3.6)*
11. **Confirm the Angleton Levee's accreditation status** and check for pending LOMRs or preliminary FIRMs. *(§4.5)*
12. Only then **re-run the comparison and issue a ranked recommendation.**
