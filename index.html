1 · Verdict, and the question that must be answered first

The four lots are likely buildable for a 5-bedroom home, and their natural elevation (reported 28–30 ft, above the estimated ~24 ft base flood elevation) is a genuine advantage. But "buildable" is not the same as "advisable," and the earlier document reached a favourable conclusion by understating cost 3–6× and omitting whole categories of expense. Corrected, the picture is:

  • Land is the small number. The all-in cost of putting a family into a house here is ~$490k–$890k on one lot, of which land ($45k–$82.5k) is under 15%.
  • Flood exposure is real and permanent. These are Zone AE parcels behind a non-accredited levee. Elevation helps; it does not remove the mapping, the insurance question, or the county's 2-ft freeboard rule.
  • The binding constraints are soil and utilities, not the flood zone — soil class decides the septic system ($8k vs $25k), and distance to power decides the service-extension cost ($0 vs $25k).
The unresolved premise The headline is “$234,500 · 4 lots,” but every comparison is written to help pick one lot to build on. These are different decisions. Buying one lot to build needs a ranked recommendation; buying four as an investment needs a carrying-cost and resale thesis. Until you decide which, no total can be sized. This document provides the inputs for both but recommends resolving the premise before spending on survey or soil work.

4 · Flood: what we actually know, and what we don't

The listing claim of "no flood risk" is false — all four lots are in a mapped Special Flood Hazard Area (Zone AE) because the Angleton Levee is not FEMA-accredited, regardless of natural ground height. That much is solid. The rest of v1's flood analysis rested on one optimistic assumption that was never justified.

The estimated BFE drives everything downstream — and it was picked from the bottom of a range.
If site BFE is…Finished floor req. (BFE+2 ft)vs natural ground 28–30 ftConsequence
24.0 ft v1's figure26.0 ft2–4 ft below gradeMet at grade. No fill for BFE. LOMA plausible.
25.0 ft (midpoint)27.0 ft1–3 ft below gradeStill met at grade.
26.4 ft (upstream cross-section)28.4 ftat/above grade on a 28-ft lotFill/stem wall needed; LOMA weaker.
The good news v1 failed to state plainly At 28–30 ft natural grade, the county's 26-ft minimum floor is not the binding constraint — build at grade and the finished floor lands near 28.5–31 ft on its own. v1's diagrams drew the finished floor below the ground, which is impossible; the reality is a comfortable margin, if the ~24 ft BFE holds per lot.
What must be confirmed in writing, per lot
  • Site-specific BFE from Brazoria County Floodplain Administration — and which cross-section governs each lot (Bastrop Bayou West Trib vs Mill Bayou differ).
  • Lowest adjacent grade from a topographic survey — not a single "28–30 ft" band applied to all four.
  • Pluvial (rainfall) ponding risk — flat clay prairie floods from rain, not just rivers; the riverine BFE says nothing about it.
  • Levee accreditation trajectory and any pending LOMR/preliminary FIRM.

Correction: the 24-in freeboard was set in 2005, not "raised after Harvey 2017." And fill + a LOMA are mutually exclusive — placing fill converts a (fee-free) LOMA into a LOMR-F, which carries a FEMA fee.

Likely system: aerobic, not conventional — and it's the biggest missing cost Brazoria's coastal-prairie clay is Class III–IV. Class IV generally cannot use a conventional drainfield, so the permitted system is probably an aerobic treatment unit (600-gal min) + spray/drip field, requiring ~2,250–4,500 sq ft plus 100% reserve, and a TCEQ-mandated maintenance contract (~$300–600/yr, forever). 10 · Fishing & amenities — corrected

Bar X Ranch offers two stocked lakes with boat ramp and pier, plus (per listings) two swimming pools, two clubhouses, tennis and basketball courts, two playgrounds and pavilions — a richer amenity base than v1 described, and the reason dues are higher than v1 assumed.

Correcting v1's "10/day · no license on private"
  • These two claims can't both be regulatory. Texas requires no licence on waters wholly enclosed within private property — and where that holds, no state bag/size limits apply either. So "10/day" can only be a POA house rule, not state law. TPWD verified
  • The private-water status is itself an open question. The exemption requires the water not be subject to overflow from public water — but these lots sit in a mapped 1%-annual-chance floodplain in the Bastrop Bayou watershed. The flood finding undercuts the fishing premise.
  • Statewide baseline (if public water): blue/channel catfish ~25/day combined; largemouth bass 5. Black drum (v1's on-site species) is brackish/saltwater — a bad data pull. Beach is ~30 min, not 20.

12 · Due-diligence sequence

  1. Decide the premise — one lot or four. Nothing else sizes until this is answered.
  2. Pull parcel IDs for all four from BCAD (account #, legal description, acreage of record, plat). v1 contained none.
  3. Confirm jurisdiction — unincorporated county vs Angleton city limits, per parcel.
  4. Request written BFE per lot from Brazoria County Floodplain & 911 Administration, 451 N Velasco Ste 210, Angleton TX 77515 · 979-864-1295 (also 281-756-1295). Ask which stream governs each lot.
  5. Resolve 750 Wagon Wheel's frontage & acreage from the plat.
  6. Order a soil/site evaluation on the shortlisted lot — it decides septic type, the largest cost swing.
  7. Order boundary + topo survey; test septic/well/lake setbacks against a real 5BR footprint.
  8. Get 3 quotes each for well, septic, and electric service extension.
  9. Get TWIA windstorm + RR2.0 flood quotes on the actual structure; price excess flood above $250k.
  10. Pull recorded restrictions for each lot's specific section + a POA resale certificate.
  11. Confirm Angleton Levee accreditation status and check for pending LOMR / preliminary FIRM.
  12. Re-run the comparison and issue a ranked recommendation.
